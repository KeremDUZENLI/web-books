### Answer 5.2.10

**a. Projection Matrices**

The normal vector to the plane $S$ is $n = (2, 3, -1)^T$. First, normalize $n$:
$||n|| = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{14}$.
The unit normal is $u = \frac{1}{\sqrt{14}}(2, 3, -1)^T$.

The projection matrix onto the normal $P_n$ is $uu^T$:
$P_n = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 6 & -2 \\ 6 & 9 & -3 \\ -2 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

The projection matrix onto the plane $S$ is $P_S = I - P_n$:
$P_S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 6 & -2 \\ 6 & 9 & -3 \\ -2 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 10 & -6 & 2 \\ -6 & 5 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & 13 \end{pmatrix}$.

**b. Sum of Matrices**
$P_S + P_n = (I - P_n) + P_n = I$. This holds by definition of orthogonal projections.

**c. Vector Decomposition**
For $x = (2, 3, 4)^T$:

- Component orthogonal to $S$: $x_{\perp} = P_n x = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 6 & -2 \\ 6 & 9 & -3 \\ -2 & -3 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{14} \begin{pmatrix} 18 \\ 27 \\ -9 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 9/7 \\ 27/14 \\ -9/14 \end{pmatrix}$.
- Component in $S$: $x_{S} = x - x_{\perp} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 18/14 \\ 27/14 \\ -9/14 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5/7 \\ 15/14 \\ 65/14 \end{pmatrix}$.

**d. Distance from $S$**
The distance is $||x_{\perp}||$:
$||x_{\perp}|| = \sqrt{(9/7)^2 + (27/14)^2 + (-9/14)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{324 + 729 + 81}{196}} = \sqrt{\frac{1134}{196}} = \frac{\sqrt{1134}}{14} \approx 2.405$.
