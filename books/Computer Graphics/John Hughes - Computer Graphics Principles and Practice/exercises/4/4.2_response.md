### Answer 4.2

**Effect on crossings:** Repeated dualization tends to shrink the polygon toward its centroid. In many cases, it effectively "unfolds" self-intersecting polygons by reducing the geometric complexity of the crossing paths.

**Self-intersecting example:** It is difficult to find a polygon where successive duals are *always* self-intersecting because the process typically drives the vertices toward a degenerate point (the centroid). However, highly non-convex "star" polygons with high vertex counts can maintain self-intersection patterns for many iterations before converging.
